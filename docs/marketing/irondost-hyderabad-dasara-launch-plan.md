# IronDost Hyderabad / LB Nagar Dasara launch plan

**Prepared:** 5 October 2026  
**Launch moment:** 20 October 2026, Vijaya Dasami / Dasara in Telangana  
**Primary launch area:** LB Nagar and adjacent east-Hyderabad neighbourhoods  
**Initial paid-media budget:** ₹5,000–₹10,000 on Meta

## 1. Executive decision

Launch IronDost as the **local, transparent, trackable laundry-and-ironing service for busy LB Nagar households**. Do not position it as the cheapest laundry company or as a pan-Hyderabad service before capacity and delivery reliability are proven.

The first campaign should sell one simple outcome:

> **“Book from your phone. We pick up, wash/iron or iron, and bring it back.”**

Use **DASARA15** as the only public launch offer: 15% off, minimum order ₹100, maximum discount ₹100, valid 10–22 October. The code and artwork already exist in the local campaign configuration; production activation and offer testing still need to be completed.

The three launch bets are:

1. **Hyperlocal trust:** LB Nagar, Kothapet, Chaitanyapuri, Dilsukhnagar, Nagole, Vanasthalipuram and nearby apartments before broad Hyderabad targeting.
2. **Operational proof:** show real pickup slots, itemised prices, order status and a real return promise in every ad and landing flow.
3. **Repeat habit:** use Dasara as the first order, then convert customers into a 14–21-day repeat laundry routine through reminders, referral credit and monthly bundles.

## 2. Market reading

The market is fragmented. Large chains such as Tumbledry, UClean and DhobiLite create category awareness with stores, apps, garment-care claims and pickup/delivery. Local operators compete through Google/Justdial listings, WhatsApp and familiar neighbourhood service. A public Magicpin scan shows 30+ laundry listings in LB Nagar, so awareness alone will not be enough.

The important customer decision is not “does laundry exist?” It is:

- Will you actually serve my address?
- What exactly will it cost per item or kg?
- When will I get it back?
- What happens if an item is missing, damaged or late?
- Can I book without calling repeatedly?

This is a frequent, moderate-pain problem. It is a strong launch category if IronDost can make the first order reliable and then create a repeat cycle. The defensible wedge is operational clarity and neighbourhood density, not a national store-count claim.

### What this means for IronDost

1. **Do not compete only on a lower price.** Large brands already advertise first-order discounts and local operators can undercut selectively.
2. **Publish a small, readable rate card.** Use per-piece prices for ironing and wash & iron if the operation is piece-based. If a service is per kg, show that unit next to the price.
3. **Make a narrower promise than the chains.** A dependable “pickup today / return by [date]” window for the first service area is more believable than “fastest in Hyderabad.”
4. **Use local density as the advantage.** A fixed pickup route for apartment clusters can reduce rider cost and improve the customer experience.
5. **Copy the proof mechanisms, not unsupported claims:** garment tagging, inspection photos where needed, itemised invoice, order status and a simple support/recovery policy.

## 4. Recommended launch positioning and offer

### Positioning

**For Hyderabad households around LB Nagar who want clean, crisp clothes without spending their evening washing or ironing, IronDost is a doorstep laundry and ironing service that makes booking, pricing and order tracking clear.**

### Brand promise

**Wash. Iron. Deliver. — without the laundry-day follow-up.**

### Reasons to believe

- Phone booking through the IronDost app.
- Prices shown before confirmation.
- Pickup and delivery windows selected in the order flow.
- Order status from pickup to delivery.
- Online payment or cash on delivery, if enabled in production.
- Support route inside the order experience.

Only use these claims if the corresponding production flow is live and tested.

### Offer architecture

**Primary offer:** `DASARA15` — 15% off, minimum order ₹100, maximum discount ₹100, 10–22 October.

**Do not activate yet:** “2 shirts + 2 pants free on first order.” The current coupon model does not implement free selected garments or true first-order eligibility. Making that claim before the pricing logic exists will create support and margin problems.

**Post-launch offer:** after a delivered first order, send a repeat-order reminder with either:

- ₹50 off the next order above a margin-safe minimum; or
- a 3-order ironing bundle; or
- a society-specific recurring pickup day.

Choose one after the first 20–30 orders reveal actual repeat behaviour and contribution margin.

## 5. Launch gates before spending on Meta

The repository has a strong customer-app foundation, but several items are still launch-critical:

1. Replace seed/sample catalogue prices. The seed file explicitly says its prices are placeholders.
2. Confirm the production service radius includes the exact LB Nagar target pockets and that unsupported addresses fail clearly.
3. Finish production API, payments, push notifications, app signing and store-review setup.
4. Test the whole journey on a physical Android and iPhone: OTP, address, quote, promotion, pickup, delivery, Razorpay success/failure, COD, cancellation and support.
5. Activate `DASARA15` in production and test minimum order, discount cap, expiry and one-use behaviour.
6. Set up Meta Pixel/Conversions API or a simple server-side booking event if traffic goes to web/WhatsApp. If the app is the only destination, use tracked links and record first-order source manually.
7. Prepare one WhatsApp operator and a response SLA: reply within 5 minutes during ad hours; no lead should wait overnight.
8. Confirm capacity: maximum daily pickups, processing capacity, rider capacity and the number of orders that can be delivered on time. Pause ads before operations break.

**If the app is not production-ready by 10 October:** launch with a single WhatsApp booking flow, a small radius and manual order confirmation. Do not send cold traffic to a broken store listing.

### ₹10,000 test plan

| Use | Amount | Purpose |
|---|---:|---|
| Cold local prospecting | ₹6,000 | Test three segments and two creative angles |
| Retargeting | ₹2,000 | Close people who watched, clicked or messaged |
| Local creator / apartment creative boost | ₹1,000 | Boost real process or community proof |
| Performance reserve | ₹1,000 | Move into the best ad set after 72 hours |

### Targeting

- Start with a 4–6 km radius around LB Nagar; expand only when pickup capacity supports it.
- Test LB Nagar, Kothapet, Chaitanyapuri, Dilsukhnagar, Nagole, Vanasthalipuram, Saroornagar and nearby apartment clusters.
- Age 23–45; all genders; English/Telugu/Hindi creative variations.
- Test broad local targeting against interest-light targeting. Meta often finds local service buyers better with broad creative than with an overbuilt interest stack.
- Retarget 7-day Instagram engagers, 50% video viewers, website visitors and WhatsApp conversations that did not book.

### Stop/scale rules

- Do not judge a creative before at least 1,000 impressions unless it is clearly broken or attracting irrelevant leads.
- Pause an ad if it produces three consecutive days of irrelevant chats or if the cost per qualified conversation is 2× the best ad with similar delivery.
- Move 20–30% more budget into a winner after it produces at least five qualified bookings, not merely clicks.
- Measure first-order CAC, not just cost per message. A cheap chat that never becomes an order is not a win.
- Set a launch CAC ceiling from unit economics: `maximum first-order CAC = first-order contribution margin × acceptable payback fraction`. Until actual costs are known, use ₹100–₹150 as a temporary test ceiling, then replace it with the real number.

## 9. Post-launch retention and referral

### First 48 hours after delivery

- Send a simple “Did everything arrive correctly?” message.
- Ask for a one-tap rating and an optional WhatsApp/Instagram review.
- Record missing-item, damage, late-delivery and price-change complaints separately.
- Ask the customer when they normally need laundry again.

### 14–21-day repeat loop

- Reminder: “Ready for your next pickup?”
- Show the last service and make “repeat this order” one tap.
- Offer a margin-safe next-order benefit, not an open-ended discount.
- Give customers a referral code only after a successful delivered order.

### Apartment growth loop

Recruit one resident champion per apartment cluster. Give them a trackable code and a fixed pickup-day option. A cluster of 10–20 orders on one route is more valuable than scattered citywide reach at the same ad spend.

## 12. Final recommendations and open decisions

### Add to the product before launch

- Serviceability result with a clear neighbourhood message.
- Exact unit beside every price.
- Confirmed pickup and return date/window in the review screen.
- Damage/missing-item escalation policy visible in support.
- Event tracking for address check, quote, checkout, payment and delivered order.
- Repeat-order shortcut after delivery.
- Referral code with fraud and one-use limits.

### Decisions the owner must confirm

- Final rates and contribution margin for ironing, wash & iron and dry cleaning.
- Whether pickup/delivery is free, minimum-order based or area based.
- Actual LB Nagar coverage and daily order capacity.
- Production app/store launch date; if not ready, whether WhatsApp is the fallback.
- Customer support phone/WhatsApp owner and operating hours.
- Damage compensation and refund rules.
- Whether the public brand uses “LB Nagar” or “L. B. Nagar” in local SEO.

### Immediate next action

Run a 10-order controlled pilot in LB Nagar before October 10. Do not spend the full ₹10,000 until those orders prove the price, pickup window, processing time and delivery promise. Then scale the best-performing creative in 20–30% increments.
