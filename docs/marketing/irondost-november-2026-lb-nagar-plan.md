# IronDost: November 2026 LB Nagar order-growth plan

Prepared 1 November 2026. This is a proposed plan, not a record of campaigns executed. The objective is to acquire and deliver profitable first orders in a small serviceable area, then earn repeat orders. Budget, capacity, actual prices, launch status and current customers are unconfirmed.

## Decision and positioning

Start in LB Nagar and a few adjacent, operationally confirmed apartment clusters. Prioritise WhatsApp and Instagram/Facebook ads, apartment referrals and Google Search/Maps. Expand into more of Hyderabad only after pickup routes and delivery quality are reliable. Target busy households, working couples and PG residents who need regular ironing and laundry.

**Positioning:** “IronDost brings laundry and ironing pickup to your doorstep in and around LB Nagar. Know your price and confirmed pickup and return window before you book.” Use this only when those capabilities work in production. Show actual clothes, people and local pickup routes. The app is a convenient booking option; completed orders matter more than installs or followers.

November includes Diwali on 8 November ([Indian Agricultural Statistics Research Institute calendar](https://iasri.res.in/images/Cal2026.pdf)). Offer pre-festival delivery only if the real return slot is before the customer needs the clothes; switch to post-festival laundry after available slots close.

## Offer and booking flow

Launch one simple **proposed** November offer only after margin and technical review: 10% off with a cap and minimum order chosen from real costs, valid through a published date. Use a new code only after creating and testing it. If true first-order eligibility cannot be enforced, state the actual eligibility and limit or omit the first-order claim. If unit economics do not support a discount, use a transparent regular-price trial instead. Do not publish an unverified turnaround, free-delivery threshold or unsupported service.

Ad/post → WhatsApp conversation or verified app link → confirm locality and serviceability → share itemised rate, minimum, delivery fee, available pickup and return slots → customer confirms → record the order and source → deliver → follow up. If the app is ready, send its verified store link; otherwise take manual bookings through WhatsApp and enter each confirmed order into a single register. Use a dedicated WhatsApp Business number, working hours and quick replies. Aim for replies within five minutes during staffed hours.

Suggested first reply: “Hi! Thanks for contacting IronDost. Please share your area or apartment, the service you need, and roughly how many items. We’ll check availability, price and pickup slots.” Do not request a full address until needed to confirm service and booking.

## Where orders will come from

1. **Meta ads:** Run click-to-WhatsApp ads on Instagram and Facebook for the confirmed LB Nagar catchment. Start with one campaign, a broad local audience and two or three creatives. If available, use the smallest practical location radius, inspect leads by actual area and exclude unserviceable locations where possible; a radius does not guarantee geographic precision. Test Telugu-reviewed, English and Hinglish text. Do not divide a small budget across many audiences.
2. **Apartment communities:** Contact 10–15 apartment managers or resident volunteers; request permission for one notice, demo or group message. Offer a shared pickup window if genuinely available. Assign a short code such as `APT01` to attribute bookings. Do not spam resident groups or promise commissions without agreeing terms.
3. **Google Business Profile:** Claim or create an eligible, accurate service-area profile, list real hours and phone number, add actual service photos and link to the booking channel. Request honest reviews after delivery; do not incentivise positive reviews or invent an address.
4. **Local referrals:** After a successful delivery, invite a customer to share a unique code or WhatsApp link. Test a fixed credit only if the team can track and fund it. Send marketing reminders only to customers who have agreed to receive them.
5. **PGs and local businesses:** Personally approach a handful of PG operators, salons and clinics for recurring laundry needs. Quote based on actual volume, service requirements and capacity. Track these separately from household orders.
6. **Local creators:** If budget permits, give one relevant local creator a real, clearly disclosed trial and a trackable booking code. Evaluate delivered orders, not follower count. Prioritise apartments if the budget is limited.

## Budget and unit economics

Illustrative 30-day tests, not forecasts. Only spend after service and booking are working. With ₹5,000: ₹3,500 Meta prospecting, ₹1,000 apartment flyers or demo materials, ₹500 reserve. With ₹10,000: ₹7,000 Meta prospecting, ₹1,500 apartment materials, ₹1,000 local creator or extra creative, ₹500 reserve. These exclude discounts, processing, rider, staff and other business costs; fund those separately. Start around ₹150–₹250/day on ads and hold back funds until the team can serve demand. Retarget only when the eligible audience is large enough; otherwise put that money toward the strongest prospecting creative.

For each delivered order calculate: customer payment excluding taxes − discount − processing − pickup/delivery − payment and packaging costs − other variable costs = contribution before acquisition. The allowable acquisition cost depends on this contribution, repeat margin and cash payback. Example only: ₹300 customer payment − ₹30 discount − ₹110 processing − ₹70 delivery − ₹10 fees = ₹80 contribution. A ₹120 cost per acquired delivered customer loses ₹40 on the first order before fixed costs; future repeat orders might recover it but must be measured. No target CAC is defensible until actual costs are known.

## Tracking and weekly decisions

One spreadsheet row per order: booking date, order ID, area/apartment, source and campaign/code, new or repeat customer, quoted value, discount, status and delivery date, actual variable cost, complaint and consent for follow-up. Avoid storing unnecessary personal details. For each channel record spend, qualified chats, confirmed orders and delivered first orders. Paid CAC = ad spend ÷ paid-attributed delivered first customers. Full acquisition cost should include creator, printing, tools and allocated labour when known. Track cancellations and refunds; avoid counting the same customer twice.

Review every week: serviceable leads, qualified chat-to-confirmed order, on-time pickup and delivery, delivered orders, complaint rate, order contribution, paid CAC and repeat orders. 30-day repeat for each cohort can only be calculated once that cohort has had 30 days. Small numbers are directional, not statistical proof. Check ad performance after 3–5 days and sufficient local conversations; pause an ad with persistent irrelevant or unserviceable leads, or acquisition costs above the agreed payback ceiling. Move budget gradually to campaigns that have produced delivered orders and maintain capacity. Pause acquisition if delivery reliability fails.
